1

Saturday 10 June, after breakfast. Mei laid the sheet flat on the table and smoothed it twice with her palm, the way she smoothed a tablecloth. It had come from Hearth, on paper, real paper, the kind they sent to households with a Keep subscription. Hearth knew the household had one, not because of a companion, there was none in this house, but because the children's drawing world was there, and the cat was there, in the corner of the frame, for nine years.

"Three things," Mei said. "Sixteen years old. Kidneys. The baby comes in August."

Wen didn't look up. His notebook was open in front of him, the pen uncapped. Coal lay on the windowsill on her side, and when she breathed her ribs rose one at a time, like a fan half folded.

"A copy isn't a cat," he said.

"I didn't say cat. I'm buying the kids one more year. Don't make a mountain out of it. I'm just saying."

Two columns on the sheet. Left: Clone. Eight months to delivery, alive, the same genes. Below it, in small print, which Wen pulled the sheet over to read: Coat pattern is not fully determined by genetics; the delivered animal's appearance may differ from the original. Right: Match. Two weeks. Hearth picks the most similar cat at a shelter and loads the model into it. A third of the price.

"August," Mei said.

Wen pushed the sheet back. He hadn't lost; he couldn't carry the carrier, and Mei couldn't bend down now. He signed.¹

2

This is how the model is trained.

For nine years the family's glasses were on, the children's drawing world was on, and Coal was in the corner of every frame. Nobody ever filmed her on purpose. She was simply there. Hearth cut her out of the edges: multi-view reconstruction for the fur, pose tracking, behaviour cloning. What the cat did, the model learns. What the cat never did on camera, the model fills in from a prior, and the prior is every cat Hearth has ever kept. So a copy leans a little toward the average cat. How far, Hearth doesn't say, and couldn't say exactly if it tried.

The training set is the cat's summary. The cut on 1 May took two thirds of the family's night footage, and when it was taken nobody thought it would be wanted.

3

The port is a veterinary one, coin-sized, behind the right ear, the same as a person's; only the licence is different. It is the animal version of Calloway's button: it reads, and it writes.

A port that only reads can't make a cat do anything. A port that writes can give the body a suggestion it cannot refuse.

The cat the shelter picked was two years old, female, calico, seven or eight parts in ten like Coal. The dark patch was over the other eye.

4

Saturday 24 June. Two cats in the kitchen.

The new one came out of the carrier, stood a moment, walked to the windowsill, jumped up, and lay down on her side. Hao looked at her, then at Coal on the floor. Coal hadn't moved.

"That's the cat from the videos," Hao said.

He was right, without knowing where he was right. What the new one did was what the recordings had kept: Coal at nine chasing the laser dot, Coal at twelve sleeping at the foot of Junie's bed. Coal hadn't done any of it in two years.

Junie, three, squatted between the two cats and tipped her head back. "Which one's mine?"

Nobody answered. Wen wrote in the notebook: Two. One does what she used to do. One stopped.

5

Three weeks.

The new one came when Junie called. The model had learned her name from the footage. Coal had never come, not once, in sixteen years.

The new one slept where Coal slept in 2041, the left arm of the sofa. Coal had stopped sleeping there in 2043, the year she couldn't get up.

Every few days the new one did something that was in no recording. She sat on the sill facing out, not in. When Wen's boots came through the door she hissed, back arched. Hearth's night note called it drift and offered a retrain, the first one free.

Mei didn't tap it. Wen wrote: The one underneath.

He didn't write further. The one underneath had a temper of her own. Had anyone asked her? Nobody had. And she had no way to say yes, or no.

6

The copy's model lived in the family's night allowance, next to Junie's drawing world. 1 May had left the drawing world ten rooms, and by the next week Junie had drawn it back, room by room, to fourteen.

The Steward asked Mei: the allowance is short, please choose.

Mei gave the cat two rooms. Junie's.

Junie didn't notice. Mei did. She stood in the kitchen a while, folded the sheet, and pushed it to the back of the drawer, with last year's check-up forms.

7

Sunday 2 July.

Junie answered her own question. "Mine's the one that comes," she said, and went to draw.

In the afternoon Wen came home and the notebook was still open on the table. Coal was lying on it, on the page that said The one underneath, asleep. She hadn't jumped onto the table in a long time. When she breathed, the paper moved.

Wen didn't move her. He stood and looked for a while, uncapped the pen, and on the strip of page beside the cat thought of writing a line. He didn't. He capped the pen, sat down, and waited.
