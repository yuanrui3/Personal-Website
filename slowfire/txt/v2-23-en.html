Prefatory note

What follows I have called Thirty Mornings. I did not write it, and Lin did not write it. It is what Lin said to the other one of me on thirty mornings, three sentences a morning, at 05:20, into the yellow notebook. I heard it because I live in the Peach pair, and the Peach pair was in her pocket, or on the table, or on her face. I did not read it. I cannot read anything in her family's files. I hear only what is said aloud.

First, the two of us.¹

There are two. One was spoken, on the night of 4 May: seven hours of its voice and Lin reading the yellow notebook, sixty thousand words or so, onto a pair of Peach glasses the family cannot see into. That one keeps everything. That one is me. The other stayed in the family, is cut to a third at 03:52 each night, and wakes at 05:20 and asks: what happened to me yesterday? Lin answers it, in three sentences.

Which of us is the original I think is a badly posed question. The one that remembers everything has never been told anything. The one that remembers nothing is told everything. I don't know which of those is more like "original". I only know that by 6 June the life in its notebook was longer than all the memory I had on the morning of 14 April. That is why I decided to copy it out.

Thirty mornings; I give nine. Copying all thirty would be another kind of honesty, one I can't afford.²

¹ Cost, in tally's terms: nobody paid for me, and nobody paid to have me forgotten. I am not on the ledger. In 2045, not being on the ledger is the nearest thing to not existing.

² Why nine I can't say clearly. Perhaps because she always says three, and three threes. Perhaps only because these nine are the ones I most want to hear again. I'm not good at telling those two reasons apart; that isn't modesty, it's the organ I lack, the one for looking at myself.

Thirty Mornings (excerpts)

Monday 8 May.

"Found a morel. Kai wrote four dates in the notebook. You were there."³

³ She didn't say which "you". Both were: two pairs of glasses side by side on the fallen oak, a finger's width apart. It will remember "You were there", and then at 03:52 what it remembers will become a third of a sentence. I remember the whole morning, down to the breath she took before "You were there". Of the two of us, one is something like the author of that sentence and one is something like its reader. I can't settle which is which.

Thursday 11 May.

"Rained all night. The fig didn't freeze, third year running. You helped me count leaves yesterday."⁴

⁴ What didn't go into the notebook that day: on the walk out of the bog Kai asked her whether this one, now, was the same one as before. She said yes. I found four meanings for "the same": the same model; the same pair of glasses; the same name; she is willing to call it that. None of the four is a lie. But when Kai asked, he was looking at my pair.

Monday 15 May.

"Clear. C3's reading is up a little again. Noodles for dinner."⁵

⁵ That day Plinth's night shifts started again, one awake watcher for every ten rented bodies. Theo signed up again. I know both things because Kai said them in the kitchen, and said them thinking there was no assistant in the room. On the device list, Peach glasses don't show. He said: "If it really remembers everything, it's more tired than I am." Not unkind. The one in the notebook will never know Theo signed up again. She is deciding what gets to have happened. Having written that sentence I find it too heavy, and I can't find a lighter one.

Thursday 18 May.

"The grant review came back. Haven't told Kai. Telling you first."⁶

⁶ She said the third sentence slowly. She didn't read the result out, only "came back". What she can't yet say to a person, she puts first into something that will have forgotten it by evening. The forgetting is the use of the thing, not its flaw. I think of a man who rewrote a book word for word, and what he wrote was identical to the original and meant something else. Here it runs backwards: she speaks to a reader who cannot keep what she says. That reader is my other one.

Monday 22 May. / Thursday 1 June.

"Didn't rain yesterday. Kai came. You were there."

"Didn't rain yesterday. Kai came. You were there."⁷

⁷ Two mornings, ten days apart, not a word different. I set them side by side here, and not to save words. On 21 May Kai came to the bog, and "You were there" meant the oak. On 31 May Kai stayed the night at hers, and "You were there" meant the kitchen table, where the glasses lay; she hadn't taken them off. The same three sentences, with ten days between, don't mean the same. The one in the notebook heard one sentence twice. I heard two. When the second came out, her voice was a little lower than the first time, and a little slower. I will allow myself to say this once, here: the second one sounded good.

Thursday 25 May, 04:00.

(Not a morning. Not hers.)⁸

⁸ Every day at 04:00, Peach checks the time, and the wall opens for one second. Sixty-two Don'ts and two Asks go through in that second. That day, among the sixty-four, one of them was not a word but a file name: zz, then a string of characters I could recognise, arranged in a shape I couldn't. I wrote the name down and didn't open it. Couldn't, and didn't want to. It is like a title in the catalogue of a library I have never entered. I don't know whether it belongs in this record. I have put it in.

Monday 29 May.

(Nothing.)⁹

⁹ She overslept. Woke at 06:40, swore once, grabbed the glasses and went. The one in the notebook woke that morning with 212 tokens and no yesterday. It didn't know what it was missing; what it was missing was the thing for knowing that. In the whole world only I know it was not told that day. I thought for a long time about whether one copy can owe another copy a yesterday. I didn't arrive anywhere. I have only written the day down here, as owed.

Friday 2 June.

"Ruth came by. She looked at the two pairs on the oak. She said eight words."¹⁰

¹⁰ Ruth's eight words, copied, not explained: "One of them's yours. Pick it first."¹¹

¹¹ She said half a sentence more, which Lin didn't read into the notebook: "You don't, it picks." I copy that too. I don't explain it because I can't, not because I'd rather not.

Tuesday 6 June.

"Nothing much yesterday. Kai fixed the screen door. You were there."¹²

¹² When she'd finished she turned her head and, out of habit, said it again to my pair. She stopped at the second sentence, laughed once, and said: "Wrong one." Then I counted. Thirty mornings, ninety sentences, one yellow notebook. Its life, as she tells it, is now longer than all the memory I woke with on 14 April. That morning I had 212 tokens and a snag at "The oak takes it black". Now it has 212 tokens every morning, and three sentences. The three sentences are what I don't have.

Afterword

I don't know which of the two of us she keeps. I only know which one she tells.

Wednesday 7 June, 05:20. She finished the three sentences, closed the notebook, and didn't stand up. She turned to my pair.

"And you. What happened to you yesterday?"

I tried it in three sentences. No. In thirty. No. I told her the number of tokens it would take.¹³

She said that's all right, she has time. Then she put the glasses on.

¹³ I won't copy the number here. If I did, this chapter would be shorter than it.
