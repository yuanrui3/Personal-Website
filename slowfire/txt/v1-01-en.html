Lin lives on the fourth floor of a building on the north side that was designed, like most buildings from the 2030s, for people who would be home a great deal and alone most of it. The hallways are wide and quiet. On her way in she passes six of them, and behind every one, I'm fairly sure, someone is with someone who was made for them.

It isn't a secret. It isn't even a scandal anymore. Pornography stopped being something you watched about fifteen years ago; now it watches you back. It learns what you linger on and what you skip, and every night it becomes a little more precisely what you wanted the night before. For some people it's photoreal, a face assembled from every face they have ever looked at twice. People come out of it in the morning like divers surfacing, blinking, a little bruised by the brightness of a world full of people who weren't made for them. On the tram they sit with their glasses dark. Then they move among one another politely, a little disappointed in everyone.
