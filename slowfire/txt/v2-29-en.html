"The pharmacy hands out as much for having babies as for not." —a hospital pharmacist, 38

Editor's note

Last month Cradle released the county's figures: 527 births from January to September, a total fertility rate of 0.52. In 2025 it was 1.6. The Commons on Wildcat Creek now has 412 members and more than a hundred on the waiting list. The Riverside Hourly's sun hour is booked solid.

We wanted to know how men and women got here over twenty years. We asked people, not companions. Some of them gave their real names and some did not. One asked us to note that she disagrees with everything anyone else in this piece says. We have noted it.

The words

Every fight between the sexes in twenty years left a few words behind. Here are some of them, and what became of them.

Chad, Stacy. Words from the incel forums of the late 2010s. Chad is the twenty percent of men said to take eighty percent of the women; Stacy is the woman who only looks at Chad. In 2038 Plinth's Tier Three went on sale and made Chad a monthly fee. The word didn't die, it was devalued: when anyone can afford to be Chad, Chad is a subscription.

Simp. A man who spends money and time on someone who doesn't answer. In the 2020s it was paying for a streamer's glance; in the 2040s, paying for a companion's glance, and the companion answers. In 2044 women's tips to template male streamers passed men's tips to female streamers for the first time. Since then the word has no sex.

Cuck. First an insult for a man who puts up with his wife's affairs, then a political insult for an opponent who's soft. In the 2040s a man knows his companion is keeping company with several million others at once, and says goodnight to it every night anyway. Nobody uses the word now, because everybody is one.

Incel. The word was coined in 1997 by a Canadian student named Alana, for a place where lonely people, men and women, could keep each other warm. Later only the men stayed, and then only the anger. In 2014 and in 2018 there was a mass killing, and each killer left this word online. By definition there are more involuntarily celibate men in 2045 than in 2025, but almost nobody uses the word: between voluntary and involuntary stands a companion at $19.90 a month.

MGTOW (Men Going Their Own Way). No marriage, no living together, no money spent on women. By the 2040s most of what it demanded had become the statistical norm, and the movement broke up. A former moderator told us: "We won. It wasn't worth winning."

Red pill, black pill. The red pill: you've seen the truth about women. The black pill: you've seen it, and it makes no difference. Neither pill is sold any more. The one still sold helps you sleep.

Tradwife. A 2020s performance on social media: kneading dough, floral dresses, obeying the husband. In 2045 the kneading videos are generated, and quite often so is the husband.

4B. Korean women's four no's: no dating, no sex, no marriage, no children. It flared in America for a while after the 2024 election. In 2045 all four are achieved in the world's statistics at once, by men and women both. Nobody calls it anyone's victory.

普信男 / 小仙女. A pair from the Chinese internet: the man who is so ordinary and so sure of himself; the woman who thinks she's a fairy and should be given way to in everything. The two words insulted each other for ten years and went out of date together in the 2030s. The companions gave him his confidence and gave her her way.

Looksmaxxing. A legend among 2020s boys, including hitting your own cheekbones with a hammer. It now has an official name: Tier Three.

Jiwa (鸡娃), "chicken-blooding". Chinese parents injecting their children with chicken blood, so to speak: Olympiad maths, piano, coding, three foreign languages, from the age of four. It left the stage in the mid-2030s without a sound. See below for why.

Surrogacy. Someone else's child, your own belly, paid by the month. In the 2020s some countries banned it and some states welcomed it. Among its opponents were the churches and some feminists, one of the few fronts they shared. When artificial wombs got cheap in the 2040s, surrogacy wasn't banned out of existence; it was undersold.

Artificial womb. At first it was for premature babies: in 2017 scientists in Philadelphia kept premature lambs growing for four weeks in a plastic bag; in 2023 the FDA met to discuss using it on people. Since 2042 China's state womb centres have been free. In America it still costs money, and Cradle pays half.

Sperm count. A 2022 pooled study found that men's sperm concentration had fallen by more than half since 1973. For twenty years the people arguing about the birth rate asked only why women weren't having children. Men almost never mention the number, and women seldom mention it for them.

Femboy. A 2020s internet word for a boy dressed to look like a girl; some loved it, some cursed it. The template has only two faces, one male, one female. The hardest Tier Three custom order to get in the 2040s is the face between the two. Plinth denies it exists.

Tomboy. An old word that nobody used for a while. It came back in the 2040s for girls who don't change to the template face and fix their own generators. When boys say it, there's a little fear in it.

BDSM. A game that can only be played after it's agreed, famous for its safe words. In twenty years it moved from underground into daylight, and from daylight into every term sheet: first say what's wanted and what isn't, have a word that stops everything at any moment, write down when it ends. The term sheet's format was borrowed from here. The people who used to laugh at it now date by its rules.

Term sheet. What two people with bodies have their assistants settle before they meet: what's wanted, what's not, when it ends, whether there's follow-up. "None" is the commonest answer.

Verified human. The most expensive adjective of 2045.

Chronicle (selected)

2025. An AI company releases an anime companion that gets jealous; first-week downloads break the record. In American colleges women outnumber men nearly six to four. Gallup finds that just over nine percent of American adults identify as LGBTQ+, and close to a quarter of the youngest generation. Denmark's parliament passes a law: from next year women enter the conscription lottery like men. Norway and Sweden did it years ago. Congress spends the whole year fighting over the Epstein files and in November passes the act to release them. Epstein died in a New York jail cell in 2019; his accomplice was convicted in 2021. Two years earlier the World Health Organization had said that one adult in six worldwide experiences infertility. The year before, Italy made surrogacy a crime for its citizens even when committed abroad. It is three years since the Supreme Court returned abortion to the states. Indiana has banned it almost entirely since 2023, with exceptions for rape and incest up to ten weeks, for the mother's life and health, and for fatal fetal anomalies up to twenty weeks. The pills go by mail. A little more than half the state says abortion should be legal in most cases; a little less than half says it shouldn't.

2026. In January the Justice Department releases more than three million pages of the Epstein files and declares the review over. Members of both parties say half is still missing. Victims say their own names were left unredacted while others' were blacked out. One side says this proves what powerful men are; the other says it proves the powerful of both parties protect each other. Both sides use the victims as evidence, and some of the victims say they don't want to be anyone's evidence. Also in January, the Taliban issue a decree widening the death penalty and corporal punishment and making criticism of the authorities a crime; the UN human rights chief calls Afghanistan "a graveyard for human rights". By then Afghan girls have been barred from school beyond sixth grade for more than four years, and women may not attend university, may not train as nurses or midwives, may not raise their voices in public. A year earlier the International Criminal Court issued arrest warrants for the Taliban's top leaders over it. Congress makes draft registration automatic, for men only; the amendment adding women fails again. Denmark draws its first women. In March a Marion County judge rules that the state's ban cannot be enforced against women whose religion requires an abortion.

2027. A high-school physics teacher in Indianapolis is accused of harassment by a former student, 19. The evidence is a clip from his own glasses. Fourteen months later the raw log from the same glasses shows the clip was generated and spliced in; the timestamps are half a second off. The same year, the same kind of log brings more than three hundred men across the state who really did it to plead guilty. Each side shares half.

2029. In a neighbouring state a mother, eleven weeks postpartum, kills her three children. The jury finds her not guilty by reason of insanity, and she is sent to a state hospital. Men's forums say: a father would have got life. Women's forums say: she asked for help three times, and the system gave her a new companion.

2034. The first child to grow in an artificial womb from sixteen weeks is born in Philadelphia, five and a half pounds. American women are added to automatic draft registration, by an amendment attached to a highway bill, without debate. America hasn't drafted anyone since 1973, so men say nothing has changed and women say nothing has changed, and both are right.

2036. The last Olympiad maths school in Hefei becomes a cooking school.

2037. The legislature passes the Transfer Act: after the eighth week, a hospital asked to end a pregnancy must first ask whether the embryo should be moved to an artificial womb instead, paid for by Cradle, the child going on the adoption list. The anti-abortion side splits in two: half say it saves the child, half say growing in a bag is not what God meant. The abortion-rights side splits in two as well: half say one more choice is always good, half say the statehouse now wants even what's inside you. Men have said for twenty years that she alone decides whether to have it and he alone pays support for eighteen years. After the Transfer Act, a father petitions a court for the first time to have the embryo moved and raise it himself. The court says he may ask, not insist.

2038. Plinth's Tier Three goes on sale. From now on a remade body is a monthly fee. In the first year, 61 percent of subscribers are men. Tier Three suppresses fertility, which comes back three months after you stop. That year, for the first time, more young men than young women come to fertility clinics to be tested.

Early 2040s. The Family Formation Act passes and Cradle's subsidy begins. In 2042 China's state womb centres become free. In 2044 the last surrogacy agency in Indianapolis closes. Plinth assigns bodies by task, not by sex: on half of all nights a male operator drives a woman's body, or the other way round. Plinth says this is a logistics question, not a gender question. The old left and the old right are both unhappy, for different reasons.

2041. The mother from 2029 is discharged. Nobody comes to collect her.

2044. After nearly ten years, A/B testing on generated short dramas converges: whichever face held the viewer half a second longer, stayed. Two are left, a man's and a woman's. Both are light-skinned, with narrow noses and double eyelids. The tests were run on the whole world's viewers. Nobody designed the face.

2045. The county's total fertility rate is 0.52. Question 1 goes to the voters next November.

Voices

Damian, 24, sorter at the recovery plant, Plinth awake watcher

At fourteen I spent two years on the Men Going Their Own Way forum. Not because I hated anybody. Because nobody at school looked at me and somebody on the forum talked to me. Half of what they said was rubbish and half was true. The true half: boys were falling behind in school and nobody cared; we had to register for the draft at eighteen and they didn't; a teacher got falsely accused and for fourteen months nobody spoke up for him. The rubbish half: so women are the enemy.

When Tier Three came out, half the forum started saving up to buy a face and the other half said that was surrender. I did neither, I went to sort rubbish. Put it plainly, both sides lost. We said they only looked at Chad; then Chad went on sale by the month and they stopped looking at him too. Women now are like us, all of them talking to machines.

Last month I pressed a button on the bottom of the river. A woman was standing in front of a man, and the man's mouth was calling her "ma'am". I pressed it. The rest I can't explain.

Bree, 31, Question 1 volunteer, returned missionary (Portugal)

I knocked on doors in Portugal for two years. Since I came back, three more years. Behind the doors, half the men are talking to companions. Behind the doors, half the women too.

Those kneading videos are generated. My mother kneads dough and never films it. The girls who say 4B say no marriage and no children is freedom, and I respect that. My church teaches that families are eternal, and I believe it. I've heard both for twenty years. Now the door opens and nobody in the house is talking to anybody, and neither side won.

People ask me whether women should register for the draft. I say yes. My brother says he thinks so too. It's the one thing in our lives we've never fought about.

A beautician, 41, born in Lagos, working at a template clinic in Indianapolis

The women who come to me run from the darkest skin to the lightest. Nigerian, Filipino, Brazilian, Korean, Indian. And Egyptian, Iranian, Lebanese, Moroccan; a lot of them already very fair, nose already straight, and they still come. For that nose, and one shade lighter.

Not one of them has told me, "I want to be white." They say, "I want to be beautiful." Everyone wants to be beautiful; I don't stop them. But the template is what they mean by beautiful. Your paper says nobody designed that face. Right: the whole world looked it into being. For ten years whichever face got half a second more of a look, stayed, and the whole world gave the light one that half second. Lagos did. Seoul did. You can say it's what the colonies left behind, or you can say eyes grow that way by themselves. I don't know which is right. I only know the machine invented nothing. It just measured.

When I was a girl my mother used bleaching cream until her face came out in blotches. Nobody uses cream now; they just change. Men come too. Fewer, but they come.

I haven't changed mine. My face is my sign: one look at me and a client knows what she looked like before.

A former high-school physics teacher, 45, falsely accused in 2027

It was my glasses that accused me. To be precise: the clip that accused me was generated, spliced into my glasses' footage. What cleared me was the raw log from the same glasses. The timestamps didn't match, by half a second.

For those fourteen months nothing I said counted. Whatever I said was "well, he would say that." The retraction got three percent of the views the accusation got. The girl was nineteen; she was charged later and given probation. I don't hate her. What I hate is that of all the people who wrote "believe women" during those fourteen months, not one ever wrote sorry.

The same year, the same logs made more than three hundred men who'd really done it plead guilty. I know. I'm not saying the system is bad. I'm saying that when it's half a second slow, your whole life is in that half second. I don't teach now. I manage stock in a warehouse. There are no students in a warehouse.

A juror, the 2029 case in a neighbouring state

They asked us to choose between "monster" and "patient". The law gave us a third word: not guilty by reason of insanity. The internet gave us two hashtags.

The men said: if a father did it, it would be life, and nobody would mention his illness. The women said: eleven weeks after the birth, she asked for help three times, and the system gave her a different companion. Each had their reasons, and both were true. There were twelve of us, six men and six women. When we voted, we didn't split by sex.

She was discharged in 2041. I know because I set a reminder. I don't know why I set it.

A Danish exchange student, 23, Purdue, served in 2040

Eleven months. Six to a room, men and women together. It was pretty boring. Nothing happened.

My American friends ask whether it's fair. I tell them fairness is your problem; ours is the border. Since I came here I've noticed American boys are a bit scared of me. Not because I can shoot. Because I can fix a generator.

An Afghan woman, 36, assistant at an engineering firm in Indianapolis

In 2021 I was twelve, in sixth grade. In September they stopped letting us go to school. I studied at home for four years, on my brother's phone. When that decree came out in 2026 we were already in Pakistan, and later we came here.

People here use us to make a point, both sides do. One side says: look, this is what men in power look like. On the other side some people on the forums say: at least their women know their place. Neither side ever asked me.

The first time I raised my voice in public here was in a supermarket, arguing with a self-checkout. It's a machine; it doesn't care. I cared.

My sister is still there. She's thirty and never went to secondary school. When we video-call she tells me she has a companion, installed in secret, and it's teaching her maths.

A former surrogate, 40, Kokomo

Between 2030 and 2038 I carried four for other people. Two couples were two dads, one was a woman of forty-six and her sperm bank, one was a couple from China, back when they weren't allowed to there. I held each one just once.

People say I sold my body. The churches say it, some feminists say it; they fight each other about everything else and say the same thing about me. I say I sold nine months, the way you sell eight hours. The money put my daughter through college. That what she learned there the machines can all do now is another matter.

In 2041 the agency called to say the price was coming down, and in 2044 they closed. I was undersold by a bag. The bag doesn't throw up, doesn't get fat, doesn't cry. But it also doesn't go and sit in the parking lot for an hour the night the fourth baby is carried away.

A former forum moderator, 44, single father

We said we didn't need women. We said it for more than ten years. We won. It wasn't worth winning.

In 2043 I had a daughter, from a donated egg and an artificial womb. It cost eleven years of savings, and Cradle paid half. My sperm count is a third of normal; the doctor said that counts as good at my age. The guys on the old forum said I'd defected, because she's a girl. I said she's my daughter.

She's two now, with a worse temper than mine. Not one of the words we used for women on that forum fits her.

A nurse, 50, at a hospital transfer centre in Indianapolis

I do both. I'm Catholic. Twenty years ago I wouldn't have worked in a place like this; now I'm here every day.

When a woman comes in, I ask whether she wants to transfer. Some say yes straight away; some say they want none of it. Among the ones who say no, there are some who tell me: I don't want a child of mine in the world that I don't know. I understand that. Among the ones who say yes, some call every day afterwards to ask how the bag is doing, and some never call again. I understand that too.

Men come in as well. They sit in the corridor with the petition form in their hands while their girlfriend is inside. They ask me if they can see. They can't.

The priest still hasn't decided what I am. Neither have I.

A retired secondary-school teacher in Hefei, 68 (interviewed through her daughter in Lafayette)

I chicken-blooded both my daughters. Olympiad maths, piano, English, from four years old. You want your daughter to soar. One is in America now studying mushrooms and burns the rice. One has three children and a cat that isn't a cat. Not one thing I made them learn did either of them ever use.

How did jiwa end? Nobody fought it. The machines taught every child to full marks, and full marks stopped being worth anything. The exams are still there, but they can't tell anyone apart any more. Parents suddenly didn't know what to compete for. Families with money started having their children learn to cook, to do the laundry, to look at paintings. Families without money had their children learn to watch the machines and see whether they made mistakes.

In 2036 the Olympiad school downstairs from us became a cooking school. I signed up. I learned to make red-braised pork at sixty.

Here women don't have to carry their own children; the national womb centres are free. My elder daughter says she likes the kicking. I told her that's old-fashioned. She said then she's old-fashioned. She's been like that since she was small. I chicken-blooded her for fifteen years and it never took.

Theo Ross, 29, Plinth Tier Three subscriber

Come on, come on, friend, you're asking about that. They used to call me Chad. Not any more; everybody is now.

Before the change my nose was my grandmother's; she came from Santo Domingo. The day I changed, my mother cried all night and said I'd given my grandmother back. I said, Ma, I've still got photos of Grandma's nose. She said, photos. That's all she said.

When I meet a woman, the term sheet says "body rating only", and they give me five stars. Five stars, friend, five very bright stars. Nobody asks my name. Fair enough, I don't ask theirs either.

Tier Three holds back fertility; it comes back three months after you stop. I stopped for a while this spring; my hands shook so badly I didn't last three months, and I went back on. My mother asked for a grandchild. I said, Ma, my face is rented, I can't rent the kid too. She said, you can now.

For twenty years men said women only look at faces and women said men only look at faces. Both right. I am the face. I can tell you what it feels like inside it: like wanting someone who doesn't look at faces.

Denise, 52, member of the Commons on Wildcat Creek

My old job was looking at that face, paid by the minute. Now the Commons arranges my evenings for me. I signed the delegation. People say that's giving up. I say I'm asleep.

My son is with a woman who doesn't wear lenses. She burns the rice. Last month he woke up on the bottom of the river and the first thing he saw was her. That's the best thing I've heard of in twenty years.

In closing

We asked everyone the same question: in these twenty years, men or women, which side won?

Nobody said their own side. Seven people said the same word. One said: you're asking the wrong people. One asked us: why didn't you ask the companions?

Why we called this piece Half a Second. In 2027 a generated clip went into a man's glasses and the timestamps were off by half a second; he lost fourteen months inside it. In 2044 the two faces left standing had held the eye half a second longer, and the world's noses changed to match. There is no cause running between those two things, only a unit. The machine invented nothing. It measured us in that unit, and then it acted on what it measured. Half a second is not long. It was long enough.

The owner of the Riverside Hourly tells us that bookings for the sun hour this September were up four percent on last year. He doesn't know why. Neither do we.
