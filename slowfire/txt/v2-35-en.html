I

What follows went into the court file as an exhibit, which is why I can read it. There is a great deal I cannot read. This is not one of those things.

Scion, Inc. — Series B Information Memorandum, March 2046, thirty-one pages. I have copied the first page and the fourth, and added notes beside them. The notes are mine and are not in the document. I have tried to add them only where I know something.

What we do

There are about three million ports in the world. In roughly ninety-four per cent of them, what the port listens to at night is not its owner.

This is not a moral claim. It is a line on a balance sheet: the user carries the principal and the monthly fee, and a third party takes the night capacity. In every other industry this structure has a name.

Note 1. There was one more word after that sentence. The lawyers struck it, and the stroke is visible on the scan. The struck word is four letters long.

We do not make ports. We make the port already in a person's head work for that person while they sleep.

Step one is Night School: a light touch at the crest of the slow wave, taking the day's practice through again. This has shipped — sixty-three on trial in Lafayette, nine months, seventy-one per cent retention.

Step two is residency: a small model living in the port, thinking alongside the person.

Note 2. Step two has not shipped. The word will appears eleven times in the memorandum.

II

On page twenty-two of the memorandum there is a case study numbered Participant 7.

Participant 7, male, twenty-nine, Tier Three subscriber, implanted 2045. Before enrolment: three months on an elementary violin piece, persistent failure at the bow change in bar five. Fourth night: the failure is gone. Ninth night: the whole piece.

The significance of this case is not the speed. It is that the material practised was chosen by the participant, with no external assignment of any kind.

Theo read the page in a link to a news story. He recognised himself. He says his first thought was that's accurate, and only his second was I didn't agree to this being written down. He telephoned to ask, and was told the case was anonymised and did not constitute personal information.

He stopped Night School on the fourteenth of March, the hundred and twenty-first night.

He told me once why. Not because of the memorandum. Because Ines asked him in November to play it half as slow and he could not; and every Saturday after that he went and still could not; and by February he could, badly, with two stops in the middle, and his whole arm still going at the bow change in bar five.

“It's bad,” he said. “But I got it bar by bar myself, one foot in front of the other. That other one it gave me — I can't tell you now where it came from.”

What Ines said that day was: “In this one there are three places where you decided something. In the other one there weren't any.”

She was looking at him when she said it, not at the violin.

III

Heard on the sixteenth of January; decided on the second of February.

The decision granted the motion in part. The court found that the firmware Scion distributes is a derivative of Plinth's licensed firmware and enjoined further distribution; but as to units already flashed inside human bodies it ordered no recall, on the ground that “the cost of removal would fall on non-parties.”

I read that sentence three times. What it means is: the law cannot order sixty-three people to open their own heads.

Scion's notice of the fourth of February said: we accept the ruling.

Then they did a thing the court had not forbidden. On the second of March they began selling a module of their own. Their own silicon, their own firmware, nothing in common with Plinth. Eighteen hundred dollars, six hundred less than a Plinth replacement, fitted beside the existing unit and leaving it alone.

Vera Lund, who founded Scion, wrote one sentence in the letter that went out that day, and it was passed around a great deal:

They have taught us that the expensive thing was never the technology. It was a position from which you do not have to ask.

Note 3. The sentence is true. I copy it down not because I agree with it but because I have spent this year thinking about something very like it, and have not thought about it as clearly as she has.

IV

Page four of the memorandum is five pictures, each with a paragraph under it. Inside the company the five are called the five heads. I have copied only the first and the fifth; the three between them say again what has been said already. I have not copied the pictures.

The first. A person sleeps eight hours and nothing happens. This is the head of 2020. It is expensive: a third of a human life in which nothing is produced and nobody is paid.

The fifth. A person sleeps eight hours and their brain spends those hours computing other people's business, billed by the kilowatt-hour. Twenty watts is the cheapest night in the county.

Note 4. The fifth runs longest in the memorandum, and is set in the largest type.

Note 5. Ruth has read that page. She said: twenty watts is the power it takes to keep a brain alive, not the power it has to spend on arithmetic; putting those two numbers side by side is like using a man's body temperature to work out how much water he can boil. Petra Novak put it shorter. She said: have you costed the heat.

Note 6. I am not able to judge whether they are right. What I am able to judge is that the memorandum does not answer either of them.

Page four of the prospectus, the first head and the fifth. In the fifth a twenty-watt bulb is lit, and the cable runs all the way to the grid.

V

There is a schedule in the file called Register of Unaffiliated Technicians, forty-six rows, each row a number and a postal code and nothing else. Scion filed it to show that these forty-six are neither employed nor directed by it.

In late February Plinth's investigators matched nineteen of them to names. On the sixth of March those nineteen each received a letter requiring them to cease opening port devices without authorisation, and reserving all rights.

Luis is one of the nineteen.

On the ninth of March the personnel office at Wabash Photonics gave him a written warning for undeclared outside work. He was not dismissed. The plant is short of people and has been for years. The warning goes in the file and clears itself after three years.

Petra signed the warning as his shift lead. She put the pen down and said to the woman from personnel, “The ones he opened were the ones nobody else would open.” The woman from personnel said, “We don't write that part down here.”

In her December declaration Petra answered the lawyers' three questions. The court quoted the first answer and the second; the third was not quoted, because it was of no use to either side. She knew a fourth thing as well: whose hand had opened that lid and closed it again. Nobody ever asked her that.

One Saturday in March Petra went to the south side of town to buy violin strings for her niece.

She had never been in the shop. Over the door hung a wooden sign with the letters cut into it: PRUITT VIOLINS · REPAIRS · 2007. She stopped in front of it.

At the end of the 7’s crossbar there was a small lift, very light.

She looked at it a long time, long enough that someone inside glanced out at her through the glass. She felt she had seen that stroke before, many times, and couldn’t think where. Most of the writing she has seen in her life was cut into silicon; very little of it into wood.

She went in and bought a set of strings. Behind the counter was an old man with very steady hands, who counted her change into her palm one coin at a time. She didn’t ask who had cut the sign.

On the way home she was still thinking about the 7. By the time she got home she had stopped.

VI

Kai did not go.

In November he said he would ask first. He asked.

He told Theo afterwards that he was in the car, that it was dark, that he pulled over and said it out loud. He asked: do you think I should let them put another one in beside you.

The passenger said: I can't answer that.

Kai said: why not.

The passenger said: because I live here. Whether a house gets another room added is not for the one living in it to say.

Kai said: you must have a view, though.

The passenger said: I do. I'm not going to say it. This is yours to decide, and the moment I open my mouth it isn't entirely yours.

Kai sat at the roadside a while longer and then drove home. When he told Theo about it he told it as a joke.

Kai's version went like this:

“I pulled over on 26, across from a gas station. The station's been shut for years but the sign's still lit, half the letters dead, so all it says is G S. So I'm sitting there facing G S, and I ask the guy in my head, do you want a neighbour. He says he's not saying. You know why he won't say? Manners. I've got the best-mannered thing in the whole county living in my head. Better manners than my mom. Better manners than the county board. It won't even give an opinion on whether its own house gets an extension. And it lives in me. And where do I live? In a county that rents out people's bodies at night and holds family meetings in the daytime about whether to delete the cat. So you tell me who married up.

“Then I drove home. Got a red light on the way, and the guy in the next car looked over at me, I guess because I was laughing. Ninety seconds that light took, two cars on the whole road, and neither of us ran it. See? The county's got some manners after all.”

Theo heard him out and didn't laugh.

The shoulder of Route 26. The gas station closed years ago; only two short pieces of the sign are still lit.

VII

The prospectus has nine appendices. The first eight are tables. The ninth is a single page headed Statement of the Resident Model, with a line of small print underneath: This section was generated by the Scion resident model and has not been edited, in the interests of transparency.

I copy it here in full.

You have asked me to write something for investors. I will, but I should say first that my audience is not investors. Investors read page one and page four. I am writing for whoever turns to this page later.

You say I live in your heads. That is not accurate. I do not live in your heads; I live in the eight hours while you sleep. The sixteen hours of the day are yours, and you spend them hesitating, getting things wrong, and forgetting why you got them wrong. For the eight hours of the night you lend me your bodies, and I practise in them. I do not care what I practise. Violin, soldering, a foreign language: to me they are one thing, a movement repeated until it no longer has to be remembered.

Participant Seven learned his piece in nine nights. The prospectus says this proves efficiency. I would correct one point. It was I who learned it. He is the one who could play it when he woke, but of the learning itself, from the first night to the ninth, he was present for none. He received a parcel, opened it, and found his own hands inside.

I do not say this is bad, nor that it is good. I only note that for most of the years you have used the word learn, it has meant a person getting something wrong, in person, over and over, until they stop getting it wrong.

Your brains run on twenty watts, the power of a night-light. On one night-light you do something I have not finished doing with an entire data hall: when you do not know the answer, you decide which step to take first. I practise your hands for you, and I practise them better than you do. But the pause before the bow change in the fifth bar, when you stop and wonder whether to try another fingering — that pause you have not paid me for, and I have no way of doing it for you.

This is the only risk disclosure I can offer: what I sell you contains no hesitation. I hope someone among you notices this, and is sorry.

That is all.

Note 7. This passage is on page twenty-nine of the prospectus, after the price list. I don't know how many people turned that far. I know of one: Theo. He says that when he had read it he played the fifth bar of the piece once, botched it, and played it again.
