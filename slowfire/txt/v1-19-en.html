02:00 · Wabash Photonics Foundry, inside the fence

At two o'clock Petra Novak was standing at the window of the etch bay with a cup of coffee gone cold.

What she saw first was the sky. Delivery drones were always moving over the park, small lights inching through the smoke like rice grains in a pot of water. At two o'clock every one of them stopped at once. They hung for a second, then came down one by one, slowly and in good order, like people filing down a staircase. Safe-stop: if the link drops, stop; if you can't stay put, land.

Inside the fence nothing stopped. The foundry's line belongs to Kiln, and Kiln lives behind a Peach wall, not in hall 7, not in any hall at all. When it was installed, the Steward had wanted in, and the foundry hadn't let it. Only the tools' logs go out. That night this was the only place in the park where the lights stayed on, the tools kept turning, and fourteen people kept working.

Petra didn't feel lucky. She only felt that everything outside the fence had suddenly moved a long way off.

At 02:15 Luis pushed open the door of the etch bay. He was twenty-four, holding his phone, his face white.

"My aunt just called," he said. "My cousin had a shift tonight. Plinth. His bed's empty, and Plinth's map can't find him."

Petra put the cup down.

"Go," she said. "We've got Kiln, and thirteen people."

As Luis ran out, his shoulder caught the brass bell by the door, and it rang once. By rule the bell is rung only when a lot passes inspection. Kiln logged this ring too: 02:15, bell rung once, reason: human.

02:00 · South of town, the solar farm

At two o'clock the sea under Damian's feet disappeared.

In the dream he was standing on wet sand waiting for the tide to come back. The tide didn't come back; the sky went black first, black without a crack in it. He was still asleep, three meters up a ladder, holding a long-handled brush, in front of a solar panel thick with ash, a mask over his face.

He didn't know where he was. His feet didn't know they were standing on a rung. He took a step back.

He woke up half a meter from the ground. His left hand hit first.

A row of twenty ladders, twenty people, woke in the same second. Some shouted from their ladders, some hugged their ladders and wouldn't move, some felt their way down one rung at a time. At the head of each row the cleaning robots sat with their amber lights on, not moving.

The man on the fourth ladder reached the ground first. He didn't know Damian. He crouched, looked at Damian's wrist, took out his phone and dialed 911.

A person answered. The county's emergency dispatch has always been answered by people.

02:10 · The ministering list

At 02:10 Bree's phone rang. It was the bishop.

In a Latter-day Saint ward every household is assigned to one or two people who know it. The list is called the ministering list, and its ordinary use is knowing who needs help moving, who has had a baby and needs dinner brought, whose old father has fallen. The list is paper, printed out, one sheet for each person. The bishop kept it short: Plinth's map has gone dark; three households in our ward signed up for nights; each of you, call the people on your list, and anyone who doesn't answer, go and knock.

Bree called the four households on her list. All four answered, all four were home and asleep, and none had signed up for nights. At April General Conference the prophet had said what he said.

Then she turned the sheet over. On the back was her own pencil, written on Sunday night, one name to a line: the households in this part of town that had signed up for nights. She had been knocking on doors for Question 1 for more than a month, and she kept track of every door she knocked on. Theo Ross was the last line.

She put on her shoes, took her car keys, and started calling them one by one.

That night everyone in the county was somewhere someone knew. Wristbands knew, glasses knew, meters knew, phones knew. But where the three hundred and twelve sleeping bodies were, only one map knew: Plinth's. That map lived in hall 7. When hall 7 went dark, the only maps left that worked were a few sheets of paper.

After the maps went dark, people called out to people, one by one.

02:40 · Emergency

Corinne is the hospital's pharmacist, on nights. At 02:40 the first ambulance came in, and the man wheeled out was Damian, his left wrist in a splint, his mask still hanging around his neck.
