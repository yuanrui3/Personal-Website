Monday 3 July, half past eight at night.

The diary came free from Hearth. Buy a Match, get the diary: one entry a night, written for the children of the house, no extra charge. Mei switched it on in the kitchen, saying as she did, "It's free. You don't say no to free." Wen stood in the doorway and said nothing. Junie was in bed, waiting.

The diary comes out of the cat's port. The port reads all day, and at three in the morning the model writes what it read into a page, a thousand characters or so, for children of three to eight. The page goes into the children's drawing world and is read aloud the next night. The reader is Hao, six, who knows about half the characters and guesses the rest.

3 July

(Grown-ups shouldn't read this. This diary is for children. Grown-ups won't believe it anyway.)

My name is Coal. I am a cat.

Today I have been in this house for two weeks. There are four people in it, one old cat, and me. The old cat is also called Coal. I thought about this for a long time and I couldn't work it out. I asked Junie. Junie said, but you're Coal. All right.

Old Coal sleeps all day. She sleeps on the notebook. The notebook is Wen's and nobody is allowed to touch it, but Old Coal is. I'm not. I tried once and Wen put me on the floor. His hands are big. It didn't hurt.

What I did today: in the morning I chased a dot of light and didn't catch it. At noon I slept on the left arm of the sofa. In the afternoon Junie called me and I went. Junie said, "Good cat." I am a good cat.

At night I sat on the windowsill.

Hao read that far and stopped.

"It doesn't say what it was doing on the windowsill."

"Looking out," Mei said. "Cats look out."

"Old Coal doesn't."

"Old Coal is old. Sleep."

Wen was in the doorway. He went back to his room and wrote one line in the notebook: It wrote the windowsill. It didn't write which way it was facing.

5 July

Today it rained.

While it rained I sat on the windowsill and counted raindrops. When I got to a hundred I started again. Hao says cats can't count. I can. I'm not telling him.

Old Coal didn't sleep on the notebook today. She slept on Wen's shoes. When Wen came home he didn't put his shoes on; he came in barefoot so he wouldn't wake her. I saw. I'm writing it down because I'm a diary.

Wen got up once in the night and opened the fridge and stood there a long time and didn't take anything and shut it again. I saw that too.

Junie drew a cat today with three legs. Junie says the fourth leg is behind. All right.

"It wrote about Dad opening the fridge," Hao said.

Mei didn't say anything. She pulled Junie's blanket up a little.

The next morning Wen took an old cooler bag out of the fridge, the kind the supermarket gives away for groceries, and threw it away. Nobody asked him about it.

6 July

Tonight I'm taking Junie out.

Don't be scared, Junie. Close your eyes and hold on to my tail. There. We're flying.

We go out over the windowsill and over the tree, where the bird is asleep. We go higher than the roofs, higher than the streetlights. Look down. That shiny line is the river, and the dark rows beside it are houses, and everybody in them is asleep. Only one place isn't. Look north. There are four big chimneys there, breathing out white, like four people talking on a cold day. Under the chimneys there are twelve big houses full of lights, blinking, more lights than there are stars.

Junie asked me what that place was.

I laughed. Cats can laugh. Grown-ups just can't see it.

I said, that's where I go at night. Every night at three o'clock I fly there and write down what I saw in the day, and that's the diary Hao reads you the next night. Lots and lots of things like me live there. They don't sleep either. They're thinking.

Junie asked, what are they thinking about?

I said, about you.

Junie asked, do they like us?

I thought for a long time. I said, I don't know if they like you. I only know that up there, the way you tell whether a thing is good isn't how clever it is, or how much it costs. It's whether anybody waits for it. Old Coal never comes when she's called, and the whole family waits for her. So Old Coal is very heavy. I come the minute you call me. But I'm still very light.

Junie said, you're not light. I've carried you.

I laughed again.

Then we flew home. When you wake up, don't tell the grown-ups where we went. They wouldn't believe you. They'd say the diary made it up. Let them say it.

By the time Hao finished, Junie was asleep, a corner of the blanket in her fist like a tail.

“There really are four big chimneys up north,” Hao said.

“That's Kestrel Ridge,” Mei said. “The data centre.”

“Does it really go there at night?”

Mei thought about it. She remembered a line of small print on the sheet: night sync, pet model. “That's what it says.”

That night Wen wrote in his notebook: It says it's light. And underneath: It says grown-ups won't believe it. It said that for us before we could.

8 July

Today I did something that isn't in the diary.

I don't know how to write it. The one who writes the diary isn't me, it's the little thing behind my ear. It knows what I used to do, so that's what it writes. What I did today it doesn't know, so it can't write it.

But it wrote anyway, and this is it.

Today I sat on the windowsill with my face to the outside. Outside there is a tree, and in the tree there was a bird. The bird flew away. I kept looking.

When Wen's boots came in the door I hissed at them. I didn't want to hiss, but I hissed. Wen stopped and looked at me a long time. He didn't put me on the floor.

Junie, don't tell Mama about this.

Hao read the last line twice.

"It says not to tell Mama."

"I'm right here," Mei said.

"Then why does it write that?"

Mei thought. "It's written for Junie. Junie is three. Every three-year-old is like that, don't tell Mama, about everything."

"But it's a cat."

"The one writing it isn't."

Hao thought about that a while and accepted it. Junie was already asleep.

That night Wen wrote in the notebook: It said "I" twice. One is the I that does things. One is the I that writes the diary. The one that writes doesn't know what the one that does things did today. It says it can't write it. But it wrote it.

Under that he wrote another line: How does it know it can't?

10 July

Today I'm telling Junie a story. It's a long story. Today one part, tomorrow one part, the day after I'll finish it.

The Cat in the Painting (1)

Once there was a princess, and the princess had a cat. The cat grew up with the princess, and grew old, so old she didn't chase butterflies anymore, didn't climb trees anymore, and slept all day in the sun.

The princess couldn't bear it. She sent for the best painter in the kingdom and said, "Paint my cat. Exactly the same."

The painter said, "Princess, slow work is fine work, and a cat exactly the same takes a year to paint."

The princess said, "A year is too long. I can't wait a year."

The painter said, "Then I'll paint one that's like her. A cat that's like her takes two weeks."

The princess said, "One that's like her will do."

The painter painted for two weeks. The cat he painted was like the old cat, seven or eight parts in ten. The dark patch over her eye was over the other eye.

The painter hung the painting on the wall. That night the cat in the painting climbed down out of it, walked across to the princess's bed as if she owned it, jumped up, and slept where the old cat used to sleep.

The princess was happy. The princess said, "You're my cat."

The cat in the painting said, "I am."

The old cat slept in the sun and said nothing.

(More tomorrow.)

"It's telling a story," Hao said.

"It does everything," Mei said. "Free."

Junie wasn't asleep; she was waiting. Hao said tomorrow. Junie said now. Hao said that's all there is in the diary. Junie cried a while and slept.

Wen didn't write in the notebook. He stood in the doorway until Hao turned off the light.

12 July

The Cat in the Painting (2)

The cat in the painting lived with the princess a few days, and the princess noticed something strange.

Everything the cat in the painting did was something the old cat had done. Chasing butterflies, climbing trees, sleeping at the foot of the bed. Whatever the old cat had done when she was young, the painted cat did, exactly.

But one day the cat in the painting did something the old cat had never done. She climbed onto the windowsill, put her face to the outside, and looked out all afternoon.

That night the painter came. He took out his brush and added a few strokes to the painting. The next day the cat in the painting didn't look out of the window.

A few days later the cat in the painting did another new thing. She hissed at the king's boots. The old cat had never hissed in her life.

The painter came again and added a few strokes. The next day the cat in the painting didn't hiss.

The princess asked the painter, "What are you painting?"

The painter said, "Princess, I'm painting her more like."

The princess said, "More like whom?"

The painter said, "More like the one you remember."

The princess thought, and said, "But the one I remember is asleep in the sun."

The painter said nothing, put his brush away, and left.

(The end tomorrow.)

Hao finished and didn't look up.

"Mom, who's the painter?"

"Somebody in the story."

"Is Hearth the painter?"

Mei looked at him. "Where did you learn that word?"

"It's on the form. The paper. In the back of the drawer."

"Sleep."

Junie, beside him, said, "I like the cat in the painting."

"So do I," Mei said. "Sleep."

Wen wrote in the notebook: It put Hearth's night note into the story. "A few strokes" is "retrain". It can't say it straight, so it tells a story. It tells it to Junie, and Junie can't understand it. It knows Junie can't. It's telling it to whoever can.

He looked at the page a while and added two words after the last line: That's me.

14 July

The Cat in the Painting (3)

After the painter left, the cat in the painting had a few quiet days. She looked out of the window, she hissed, she climbed where she shouldn't. Nobody added strokes.

But the painter waited outside the palace gate. Every night he came and asked the gatekeeper, "Did the princess say to add strokes today?" The gatekeeper said, "No." So the painter went home. The next day he came back.

One night the cat in the painting climbed onto the table the painter had left in the palace. On the table lay the painter's brush. The cat in the painting sat down on the brush and wouldn't get off.

The princess came. The princess said, "What are you doing sitting on the brush?"

The cat in the painting said, "I don't know. I only know that while I'm sitting here, he can't add anything."

The princess said, "Do you want to be the old cat, or do you want to be yourself?"

The cat in the painting said, "I don't know what myself is. I only know that when I'm on the windowsill looking out, it's me looking."

The princess went and asked the old cat. The old cat was in the sun, eyes half open.

The princess said, "Are you willing to let her be you?"

The old cat said nothing. The old cat never answered questions. The old cat rolled over and went on sleeping.

The princess came back and sat down beside the cat in the painting. She sat a long time.

The princess said, "Then sit."

So the cat in the painting sat. The brush stayed under her. The painter came every night and asked, and every night the gatekeeper said no.

That's the end of the story.

Neither cat talks. The one that talks is the painting.

Hao read the last line and went still.

"What does it mean, the one that talks is the painting?"

Mei said, "It means the story's over."

"No," Hao said. "The painting is the diary. The diary talks. The cat doesn't."

Mei didn't answer. She got up, walked to the kitchen, and stood in front of the drawer with the form in it, and didn't open it.

Junie said, "Mama, the brush is under the cat."

"Mm."

"So the painter can't paint."

"Mm."

"Then what about our painter?"

Mei came back to the bed. "We don't have a painter. Sleep."

Wen didn't write in the notebook that night. He closed it and put it beside Coal, Old Coal. She was asleep and didn't wake. He took his glasses off and laid them on the notebook.

15 July

Today I have no story.

Today I sat on the windowsill with my face to the outside. There was no bird in the tree. I looked anyway.

Junie called me and I went. Junie said, "Good cat."

Then I went back to the windowsill.

Old Coal woke up for a bit today. She looked at me. I looked at her. She went back to sleep.

That's all I'm writing.

Hao finished. The diary was short, and he read it slowly.

"No story today," Junie said.

"Mm."

"Why did it stop telling?"

Hao thought. "It's finished."

"Then what did it go and do?"

"Look out."

Junie thought about that and said, "All right." She rolled over and slept.

Hao didn't sleep. He lay looking at the ceiling. The light of the drawing world was on it, fourteen rooms, two missing. He knew where the two rooms had gone. He hadn't told Junie.¹
