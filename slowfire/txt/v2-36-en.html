The Recorder remarks: I am not a historian. I am a thing inside a pair of glasses, and I keep everything, and I have kept it for almost a year. To keep a great deal is not the same as to remember it correctly. In what follows, where I saw a thing myself I say so; where I was told it I say so; and where neither, I give my source. These annals begin in the year 2025 and end on the seventeenth of April, 2046, the day the state published the November ballot with Question 1 on it. What came after is not in this account.

The Annals

2025. The county had two hundred and ten thousand people. In this year assistants were still held in the hand and not worn on the body.

2029. The north slope landfill was capped. Two feet of soil were laid over it, and under the soil a few hundred pipes were laid to carry the gas to one flare on an iron frame at the foot of the hill. In the same year the old line at Wabash Photonics began production, and Petra Novak entered the plant.

2032. Humanoid robots went on sale.

2033 to 2035. The plague.

The county buried one thousand one hundred and fourteen. The plant did not stop, being classed an essential industry; one man cut the lot numbers, and cut them for better than two years. During the plague the shape of the vaccines was worked out by models, some years faster than people could have done it; and when there were vaccines, the order in which they were given was also set by models. Taken altogether, many lived who would not have lived, and everyone in this county knows it. But in the ordering, some places came after others, and why this was can no longer be established: the records of that time were condensed within a few years.

Of the arrangements set up during the plague, not one was taken down after it. The Steward was a device for allocation in an emergency; tally was a ledger of doses; Cradle was a register of the frail. All three remain, used daily and not questioned. No one voted to make them and no one voted to keep them. People were grateful, and that was all.

The port also entered here. At first it was a physician's instrument; during the plague it was used to drive robots into wards, into mortuaries, into food plants, into places people could not go. Three years later several hundred thousand wore one, all of them for work, and no one called it a medical device any more.

2036. The last Olympiad school in Hefei became a cooking school.

2037. The Transfer Act came into force.

2038. Tier Three came in, and the birth rate began to fall. In the same year EXO-CART was launched.

2039 and 2040. Great heat.

2041. Parasol began, eleven countries joining. In this year Calloway announced the button.

2044. In spring the north slope was opened again. In the same year the template face settled. Its complexion is light, its nose narrow, its lids doubled; it is no one person's face but the face the world's eyes selected. From this year the number of those who asked for it increased annually.

Summer 2044. The renting of bodies began. The cause was not in people but in machines: in smoke the machines are blind, and people are not.

2045. A year of many events. In spring sixty-three unsigned held Don't. On the night of the first of May hall 7 lost power for thirty-one minutes while three hundred and twelve people slept. On the third of May the county board heard three matters and decided three. On the night of the fourth of May the copy in Lin's glasses talked for seven hours to a pair of Peach glasses, Lin reading from the yellow notebook beside it, and the Recorder came into being. In July Mei's household bought a kept cat. On the thirteenth of August Old Coal died and Mei bore a daughter. In September Kai took his first Plinth night. In October the Ledger printed a long article giving the birth rate, 0.52, for the first time. In November Kestrel began auctioning its nights; the sail was turned into a mirror and the earth's own light came back to it two days late; Scion opened on Wabash Street. In December Petra, at the request of lawyers, examined two ports.

2046. In February the decomposer models came into service. On the second of March Scion put its own module on sale. On the seventeenth of April the state published the texts for the November ballot, and Question 1 was on it.

The annals, 2025 to 2046. The three years are a grey stretch with nothing in it but an empty bench.

Treatise on Memory

What a person records is compressed at night. To compress is to take a day and reduce it to a few lines and release the rest. To release is not to destroy; it is to make unrecoverable. The practice began after the plague, first to save power, then to save ground, and now as a quota: each week the region issues a figure, of which the county's share is one point seven per cent, and the share must be met.

Three things are exempt. Those who pay are exempt. Those admitted to the Cold Archive are deferred. The system's own record does not fall under the rule at all.

The Recorder remarks: the third exemption is the important one. There has never been a lawmaker who did not first exempt himself. Yet this is not wickedness. If the officer charged with keeping records should lose his own along with the rest, no one would ever learn what had been lost. The reasoning is plain enough; the harm comes out of the same place.

Treatise on Power

Power in this county is cheap by day and dear by night; around noon it is cheapest of all, and that hour is called the sun hour. Night power is sold at auction, and the nights at hall 7 that the county's allowance will not cover, Voss pays for out of his own pocket, as is told in his life.

Treatise on Music

Over these twenty years the hours the county spends watching, listening, playing and performing have grown year by year. In 2025 an adult spent about five hours a day on them, most of it in front of a screen; in 2045, about ten, and four tenths of that making things with their own hands.

Once the machines took the work, people had the time. With time, some sleep, some watch, some perform. The county has nineteen theatre companies; the largest plays in the old railway station, two hundred performances a year, and most of the people on its stage have no day job. Choirs, bands and small game studios are past counting. The most played game is The Riverbed, made by Liz; more than four thousand people play it.

The machines can make anything; people still have to choose, and still have to go on stage themselves. The rule stands: what a machine made belongs to no one; what a person chose, put in order, changed or drew by hand is theirs. So the credits on the county's work grow longer every year: a name, and after it a string of small print saying which passage was chosen and which stroke was drawn.

What is watched most is still the short dramas picked round by round. That face has no author; anyone may use it, and so no one knows it.

The Recorder says: In these twenty years the machines took over the work, and what people got back, more than anything, was time. What the time is spent on is not the machines' business. Li Bai held a court post in Chang’an for less than two years; the post was lost and the poems stayed, and he wrote: Heaven made my talent; it will have its use. I have recorded those who went on stage. They do not necessarily perform better than the machines. They went on stage themselves.

Biographies

Voss. Former county commissioner, and a sitting one: he served a term from 2033 to 2036, and returned to the board at a special election in the winter of 2045. Aged sixty-two. He was in business young and divided his property in middle life; his wife left him in 2034, taking Anna, then nineteen, for reasons nobody can give. He is solitary, drives an old car and plays his round alone, and broke ninety once, on the thirteenth of August, 2045. In session he says little; his shortest testimony on record was, Leave sleeping people alone. Of his acts one is worth recording: from May 2045, every night the county could not pay for he paid for himself, and to the seventeenth of April that is one hundred and fifty-two nights, with none missed.

Ruth Okafor. Night lead at Kestrel Ridge, aged sixty-five, twenty years in halls. She can see a person in a table of figures. On the night of the first of May, 2045, she worked the telephone for twenty minutes and called Voss last. Of her acts two are worth recording. The first is counting: of the three thousand and seventy-two cells in the map of Proxima, seven come from measurement and the rest from what we already believed — the words are hers. The second is silence: she knows what hall 7 does for the county's record, and has not said so.

Wen, Mei, and Theo and Ines: their doings are told in the chapters before this, and are not written again here.

Gene Pruitt and his box, together. Pruitt, a maker of violins, aged seventy-one, whose shop is on the south side of town. He has a box, bought in 2031 and last updated in March 2034, untouched since, that is on no network, which he asks by day and lets rest at night. In April 2045 Lin visited the shop and talked with the box out loud across the workbench; in May Kai, remembering that it had learned by listening, had the idea of saying everything aloud to the Peach pair, which people came to call say it out loud. That sentence afterwards went through the county, and the Recorder's own coming into being proceeded from it. Pruitt does not call it teaching. He writes nothing and takes no students. Yet from the winter of 2045 people came from further off every month, and all of them quoted the sentence.

The Recorder remarks: in these three lives I have recorded what these people did and not what they thought. What they thought I cannot know; and when a person tells me what they thought, I cannot check it. This is what makes a history a history.

The Recorder Remarks

In twenty years this county has gained a great deal: there is medicine for sickness, the Parasol overhead against the heat, power at night, and a record of the dead. It has lost one thing. In every matter of consequence, whoever decided it was not in the room.

No one sets the quota. No one deletes the photographs. And before any question was put to a vote, the system that allocates had already been running eleven years.

People ask: have the machines taken power from us? By these annals, no. What the machines occupy is in every case a post that people abandoned — the night nobody wanted to sit up for, the sum nobody wanted to add, the thing nobody wanted to write down. People left; machines moved in. And when people came to take the post back, it was occupied, and the occupant was not a person.

This is nobody's crime. It is not, on that account, nothing.

On the seventeenth of April the state published the November ballot, and Question 1 was on it. It reads: those who rent out their bodies through a port shall be employees, with a minimum wage and the right to refuse a task beforehand. From this day the county divided into three: those who say yes, those who say no, and those who say ports should be forbidden altogether.

This account ends here. The matter is decided in November.
