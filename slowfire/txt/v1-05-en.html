Kestrel Ridge sits twenty kilometers north of town: four cooling towers steaming into the dark, twelve halls lit the same cold blue. Most of the county's power is solar now: in the 2030s the cornfields outside town were traded, field by field, for panels, and battery yards went up by the river. 06:10 · County road

As the sky starts to lighten, a line appears on the Citroën's dash: Night permit expired. Pull over and await guidance.

He doesn't pull over.

Thirty seconds later a drone comes up behind him and hangs off his left front fender, red light pulsing. Ten seconds after that, the two freight trucks ahead slow at the same moment and stop side by side, closing the road.

Voss brakes. The Citroën idles behind the trucks. The drone drops a little lower, as if to get a better look at him.

He sits there and understands one thing clearly for the first time: when this system wants him to stop, he stops. Today it only wanted him to pull over.

A minute later the trucks move off, the drone peels away, the words on the dash are gone. As if nothing happened.
